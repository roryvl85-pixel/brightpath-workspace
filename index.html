Read the full story

Before Signal & Script, I spent seven years as an ISO 9001:2015-certified auditor at Anglo Platinum, embedded in the maintenance and repair processes for some of the largest and most expensive mining equipment in the world — excavators, drill rigs, and haul trucks from Caterpillar and Komatsu, crushers from FLSmidth and Metso, pumps and vibrating screens from Weir Minerals, and the repair of the massive hydraulic cylinders that power this equipment. I audited the companies responsible for repairing it, holding their processes to a level of scrutiny most industries never see.

That scrutiny exists for a reason. In mining and heavy engineering, tolerances are measured in fractions of a millimetre, and the cost of a process failure isn't just downtime — it's equipment worth millions, and in some environments, real physical risk. There's no room for "good enough." After seven years in that world, I spent two more years running my own consultancy doing the same work independently.

Before I ever audited a process, I worked inside one. I started my career as a piping fabricator and worked my way up through foreman, supervisor, QC inspector, senior QC inspector, and QC manager — I've seen how a process actually runs from the factory floor, not just from a clipboard.

Most of the businesses I work with now are simpler to map than a mining process — fewer moving parts, fewer handoffs, fewer people involved. That's good news: it means less to untangle, and a faster path to a clear picture of how your business actually runs. Your expertise stays yours — a dentist's clinical skill or an attorney's legal judgment isn't something I touch. What I look at is everything around that expertise: how a client or patient moves from first contact to being helped, and where that journey gets slower, less consistent, or harder than it needs to be.
